2. Executive Summary

An Equb (ዕቁብ) is a traditional Ethiopian Rotating Savings and Credit Association (ROSCA) that serves as one of the most prominent informal financial mechanisms in Ethiopian society. In an Equb, a group of trusted participants agree to pool fixed periodic contributions (daily, weekly, or monthly) into a collective fund. At each scheduled cycle, the accumulated sum is distributed as a payout to one selected participant according to predetermined rules (such as a lottery draw, seniority ranking, or mutual auction).

Despite handling hundreds of millions of Ethiopian Birr (ETB) annually across urban and rural demographics, traditional Equb operations rely overwhelmingly on manual bookkeeping: paper registers, physical notebooks, handwritten receipts, and unorganized SMS/Telegram chat logs. This reliance on informal manual administration causes frequent bookkeeping errors, misplaced records, payment status disputes, lack of transparent member oversight, and vulnerability to unilateral misappropriation by organizers.

The EthioEqub Digital Management System is a professional, secure, role-based web application engineered to digitally manage and govern Equb group operations end-to-end. The system digitizes membership onboarding, automated contribution scheduling, manual payment recording with transaction reference tracking, dual-control payout authorization workflows, dispute management, transparent audit logging, and downloadable financial statements.

Core Financial Boundary & Integrity Principle
In Version 1.0 (v1), the EthioEqub system does not hold custody of user funds nor directly execute automated bank debits. Money continues to flow through established external channels (cash, commercial bank transfers, Telebirr, and CBE Birr). The system serves as the definitive, immutable digital ledger of record that reconciles and audits all commitments, receipts, payouts, and adjustments against verifiable transaction references. All simulated or test data is strictly isolated and clearly flagged.

3. Background and Problem Statement

3.1 Societal & Cultural Background

In Ethiopia, informal financial institutions—chief among them Equb (savings/credit) and Iddir (bereavement/insurance)—predate the formal banking system and maintain deep cultural legitimacy. For millions of micro-entrepreneurs, civil servants, merchants, and diaspora families, Equb provides vital access to lump-sum capital without the collateral hurdles, bureaucratic paperwork, and high interest rates associated with conventional commercial banks.

Equb arrangements range from small family circles (5–10 members contributing 500 ETB weekly) to massive commercial market associations in Addis Ababa (50–100+ merchants contributing 20,000–100,000 ETB daily or weekly). Equb operations are typically managed by a leadership group consisting of a Chairman/Organizer (Sebsabi / ሰብሳቢ), a Treasurer (Genzeb Yazhi / ገንዘብ ያዥ), and a Secretary (Tsehafi / ጸሐፊ).

3.2 Problem Analysis

Through field observations and stakeholder interviews with Equb organizers and participants, five core systemic problems were identified:

  • Vulnerability and Physical Loss of Records: Physical ledgers deteriorate, become water-damaged, or get lost during changes of leadership. If a notebook is misplaced, entire multi-month payment histories and outstanding debt balances are obliterated.
  • Payment and Balance Disputes: Members frequently assert they paid via cash or bank transfer on a specific date, while the treasurer’s notebook indicates a pending or delinquent balance. Without a centralized, verifiable reference lookup, disputes can destabilize the group or dissolve friendships.
  • Unilateral Control and Lack of Dual Control: In traditional setups, a single treasurer decides when payouts are disbursed, creating opportunities for delayed payments, favoritism, or outright embezzlement without independent counter-signatures.
  • Bookkeeping Overhead and Calculation Inaccuracies: Organizers spend hours manually tallying who paid, who owes late fees, who has already collected their payout, and how much remains in the collective pool. Fractional errors and miscalculations multiply across cycles.
  • Lack of Real-Time Member Transparency: Ordinary members have no visibility into the current pool status, other members’ compliance rates, or upcoming payout queues unless they attend physically or request verbal updates.

3.3 Formal Problem Statement

Problem Statement
Traditional Equb associations in Ethiopia lack a secure, accessible, and verifiable digital management system that preserves the cultural ethos of community savings while eliminating bookkeeping errors, record loss, unauthorized payout disbursements, and payment disputes through automated scheduling, dual-approval controls, and an immutable audit trail.

4. Project Objectives and Expected Benefits

4.1 General Objective

To design, engineer, verify, and document a secure, production-ready, full-stack digital management system that automates the administration, financial recordkeeping, payout governance, and auditing of Ethiopian Equb associations.

4.2 Specific Objectives

  1. Group & Rule Configuration: Enable Equb organizers to configure groups with customizable frequencies (daily, weekly, monthly), contribution amounts, member counts, and payout rules.
  2. Automated Cycle Scheduling: Automatically generate mathematically consistent contribution cycles and payout schedules upon group inception.
  3. Immutable Financial Recording: Provide treasurers with a streamlined interface to record member payments (cash, bank transfer, Telebirr, CBE Birr) with required transaction reference numbers and zero in-place ledger deletions.
  4. Dual-Control Payout Governance: Implement a mandatory two-person approval workflow (Treasurer + Secondary Approver) before a payout is recognized as disbursed.
  5. Real-Time Member Self-Service: Provide members with a responsive dashboard displaying their personal payment history, outstanding balances, upcoming deadlines, and payout queue position.
  6. Comprehensive Financial Reporting: Generate downloadable periodic reconciliation statements (CSV/PDF) reflecting total collected funds, disbursed payouts, and outstanding arrears.
  7. Tamper-Evident Audit Logging: Capture a permanent, append-only log of every financial creation, status modification, dispute, and administrative action, including actor identity, timestamp, and IP address.
  8. Pedagogical Excellence: Structure the codebase and documentation to serve as an exemplary model of clean software engineering for a student developer, adhering to strict phase-by-phase verification.

5. Project Scope and Limitations

5.1 In-Scope Capabilities (Version 1.0)

  • User Authentication & Profiles: Email/phone registration, password hashing (Argon2id/bcrypt), role-based authorization, session management via HttpOnly cookies.
  • Group & Membership Lifecycle: Creation of groups, rule specification, member invitations, role assignments (Admin, Treasurer, Manager, Member), and member deactivation.
  • Cycle & Schedule Engine: Automated calculation of contribution deadlines, cycle states, and payout dates based on frequency and start date.
  • Financial Ledger Operations: Manual entry of contributions with payment channel categorization (Cash, Telebirr, CBE Birr, Bank Transfer, Other) and unique reference logging.
  • Adjustment Accounting: Prohibiting hard deletions; correcting data entry mistakes via signed adjustment entries with audit reasons.
  • Dual-Control Payouts: Two-step approval requirement for disbursing pooled funds to cycle recipients.
  • Dispute Management: Mechanism for members to flag a disputed transaction with an admin resolution workflow.
  • Dashboards & Analytics: Visual statistics on pool collection, delinquent members, upcoming cycles, and payout progress.
  • Reporting Engine: Exportable CSV ledger tables and downloadable PDF account statements.
  • Notification Infrastructure: Email notification dispatch for due dates and receipts, with an architectural abstraction ready for SMS integration.

5.2 Out-of-Scope Capabilities (Deferred to Future Versions)

  • Direct Payment Gateway Settlement: The system will not process live automated debit/credit card charges or direct Telebirr/CBE API API merchant wallet withdrawals in v1.0. (Reason: Requires commercial financial licenses, banking merchant contracts, and capital reserves).
  • Escrow / Fund Custody: The software platform never holds legal custody of user funds.
  • Native Mobile Applications: No native Swift (iOS) or Kotlin (Android) apps. The frontend is built as a fully responsive Progressive Web Application (PWA) compatible with mobile browsers.
  • Multi-Currency Cross-Border Forex: Default currency is Ethiopian Birr (ETB). Multi-currency conversions are deferred.
  • Micro-Lending / Interest-Bearing Loans: Traditional Equb is zero-interest. Secondary auction bidding rules will be supported structurally in logic, but complex compound interest calculations are out of scope.

5.3 Explicit Project Constraints

Operational Constraints
  • Connectivity: Target users operate on varying mobile internet connectivity. API payloads must remain lightweight (< 50 KB) and optimized for low latency.
  • Cost Constraint: As an academic final-year software engineering project, all infrastructure must operate seamlessly on zero-cost tiers (Render, Neon, Vercel, Supabase) without requiring paid cloud subscriptions.
  • SMS Notification Fallback: Ethiopian SMS gateway aggregators (e.g., Afromessage) require registered business licenses. In development and academic demonstration, Email and simulated in-app notifications serve as the primary verified delivery channel.

9. Business Rules for Equb Operations

Business rules define the operational policies, constraints, and financial calculation invariants of the Equb domain. Every rule must be strictly enforced in the backend domain layer.

BR-001: Integer Minor-Unit Currency Representation
All monetary amounts must be stored and computed as 64-bit integers in minor units (cents / santim). For Ethiopian Birr (ETB), 1 Birr = 100 santim. (e.g., 5,000 ETB is represented as 500000). Floating-point data types (FLOAT, DOUBLE) are strictly prohibited for financial balances to prevent IEEE-754 precision rounding errors.
BR-002: Cycle Count and Member Synchronization
The number of cycles in an Equb group equals the number of full membership shares ($N$). Each full member is entitled to receive exactly one payout during the complete Equb round. If a member purchases a fractional share (e.g., 0.5 share), they must be paired with another fractional member to constitute one full payout cycle, or receive half the pooled payout.
BR-003: Contribution Lifecycle & Late Payment State Engine
A contribution record follows an explicit state transition machine:
PENDING → PAID (full amount received on/before due date)
PENDING → PARTIAL (partial amount received; balance remains pending)
PENDING → LATE (due date has elapsed without required contribution)
PAID / PARTIAL → DISPUTED (member challenges the recorded record)
BR-004: Payment Method Classification & Mandatory Reference
Every contribution record must specify an authorized payment channel: CASH, BANK_TRANSFER, TELEBIRR, CBE_BIRR, or OTHER. For all electronic channels (Bank, Telebirr, CBE Birr), a unique external transaction reference number (e.g., Telebirr transaction ID or CBE slip number) is mandatory. Cash transactions must record the treasurer's physical receipt book number.
BR-005: Duplicate Reference Collision Prevention
The system shall enforce a unique composite constraint on (group_id, payment_method, reference_number) for all non-null references. Any attempt to record a contribution using an already recorded reference within that group shall be rejected immediately with an HTTP 409 Conflict.
BR-006: Immutable Ledger & Adjustment Accounting
Under no circumstances may an existing financial contribution row be edited or deleted (no in-place UPDATE or DELETE). If a treasurer records an erroneous amount or assigns it to the wrong member, the correction must be effected via an Adjustment record specifying the delta (positive or negative), the correction rationale, and the authorized actor ID.
BR-007: Dual-Control Payout Authorization
Disbursement of a cycle payout pool requires independent authorization from two distinct privileged users: Approver 1 (typically the Treasurer verifying fund availability) and Approver 2 (the Group Owner or Manager confirming member eligibility). The system shall reject any approval where Approver 1 ID == Approver 2 ID. Phase 2 Decision: For small informal or family groups (< 5 members), the Group Owner may configure single-approver disbursement at group creation if enabled in group bylaws.
BR-008: Payout Sequencing Algorithms
The order of payouts is established at group formation through one of three transparent algorithms:
  1. Pre-Agreed Seniority (JOIN_ORDER): Sequential schedule based on agreed roster or initial join date.
  2. Verifiable Lottery Draw (LOTTERY): Randomized selection executed at cycle inception, recorded in the audit log.
  3. Auction / Bidding (AUCTION): In groups that permit bidding, members bid a discount rebate to claim an urgent payout. The discount is distributed as a dividend among remaining members.
BR-009: Group Dissolution & Freezing Rules
An Equb group cannot be deleted while active cycles or outstanding balances exist. An owner may only mark a group as FROZEN (suspending all writes during dispute resolution) or CLOSED (upon completion of all cycles and full zero-balance reconciliation). Historical data remains permanently accessible read-only.
BR-010: Dispute Freezing and Resolution Protocol
When a member raises a dispute against a recorded contribution:
  • The contribution status immediately transitions to DISPUTED.
  • The disputed amount is withheld from aggregate payout pool calculations until resolved.
  • Resolution requires an Administrator or Owner to submit a formal investigation finding, triggering either an adjustment entry or reaffirmation of the original record.
BR-011: Strict Non-Negative Balance & Overpayment Rules
Overpayments by members are permitted and credited to an unallocated prepaid balance. In subsequent cycles, the prepaid balance is automatically drawn down to satisfy new contribution demands. Negative contributions are strictly forbidden.
BR-012: Complete Audit Context Capture
Every mutating API request on a financial entity must extract and persist: (1) Actor User ID, (2) Client IP Address, (3) User-Agent Header, (4) Precise UTC Timestamp, (5) Action Type, and (6) Pre- and Post-Mutation JSON state diffs.
BR-013: Member Replacement and Default Guarantee (Wasa)
If a member defaults after having already received their payout, the traditional Equb rule requires their assigned guarantor (Wasa / ዋስ) to assume contribution liability. Phase 2 Decision: Guarantor assignment is configurable per group: strictly mandatory for members receiving early payouts if enabled in group bylaws (protecting the collective pool post-disbursement), and optional for other cycles.
BR-014: Cycle Pool Solvency Invariant
A payout cannot be released if the total confirmed collected contributions for that cycle do not equal the required pool sum, unless the group explicitly authorizes an advance loan from group reserve funds, which must be audited as a reserve draw.

UC-01: Configure Equb Group and Generate Schedule

  • Actor: Group Owner (Sebsabi)
  • Pre-conditions: Owner is authenticated and holds verified email credentials.
  • Main Flow:
    1. Owner navigates to "Create New Equb" screen.
    2. Owner enters Title ("Bole Commercial Equb Round 3"), Description, Contribution Amount (10,000 ETB), Frequency (Weekly), Number of Members (20), Start Date (2026-11-01), and Payout Rule (JOIN_ORDER).
    3. System validates inputs (amount > 0, members >= 2, date >= today).
    4. System renders an interactive preview of all 20 calculated cycles with projected due dates and payout dates.
    5. Owner confirms creation. System executes an atomic transaction creating the Group and 20 Cycle records.
    6. Owner invites 20 members via email/phone link.
  • Post-conditions: Group status set to ACTIVE; schedule persisted; invitations queued.

UC-02: Record Member Contribution with Reference Proof

  • Actor: Treasurer (Genzeb Yazhi)
  • Pre-conditions: Group is active; cycle is in OPEN state; member is registered in group.
  • Main Flow:
    1. Treasurer selects current cycle and chooses Member from roster.
    2. System displays member's current expected balance (e.g., 10,000 ETB).
    3. Treasurer inputs received amount (10,000 ETB), payment method (TELEBIRR), and reference number (TB-9847291048).
    4. System checks database for reference uniqueness within the group.
    5. Treasurer clicks "Submit Contribution".
    6. Backend validates payload with Zod, checks role authorization, persists Contribution row inside a transaction, and writes an unmodifiable AuditLog row.
    7. Dashboard updates instantly; an automated email receipt is dispatched to the member.
  • Alternative Flow (Duplicate Reference): If reference TB-9847291048 exists, system rejects write with HTTP 409 and displays error: "Transaction reference already recorded for this group on 2026-10-15."

UC-03: Dual-Control Payout Authorization and Issuance

  • Actors: Approver 1 (Treasurer) and Approver 2 (Group Owner / Manager)
  • Pre-conditions: Cycle collection deadline has arrived; total pool is verified; payout recipient is identified.
  • Main Flow:
    1. Treasurer opens "Payout Approvals" screen, reviews Cycle 4 recipient (Abebe Kebede, Pool: 200,000 ETB).
    2. Treasurer confirms all 20 member contributions are reconciled and clicks "Authorize Payout (Approver 1)".
    3. System updates Payout record status to PENDING_SECOND_APPROVAL, recording Approver 1 user ID and timestamp.
    4. Group Owner receives an approval notification, logs into dashboard from their separate device.
    5. Group Owner reviews payout details and clicks "Finalize Disbursement (Approver 2)".
    6. System verifies Approver 1 != Approver 2, marks payout PAID, logs disbursement date, and updates group pool ledger.
  • Post-conditions: Recipient notified; payout marked complete; audit log permanently updated.

16. Authentication, Authorization, Privacy, Security, and Audit Requirements

16.1 Threat Modeling & Countermeasure Framework

Threat Vector Potential Exploitation / Impact Implemented Countermeasure
Credential Stuffing & Brute Force Automated scripts attempting dictionary passwords against user logins. Express rate-limiting (maximum 5 failed login attempts per 15 minutes per IP); strong password entropy requirements; Argon2id hashing.
Cross-Site Scripting (XSS) Malicious scripts injected via notes stealing auth tokens from LocalStorage. Auth tokens stored strictly in HttpOnly, Secure, SameSite=Lax cookies inaccessible to JavaScript; React auto-escaping; Helmet CSP headers.
Cross-Site Request Forgery (CSRF) Forged requests triggering unauthorized contribution or payout actions. SameSite cookie flags; custom Anti-CSRF header verification (x-requested-with) on all state-changing mutating verbs.
Broken Object-Level AuthZ (BOLA / IDOR) User altering URL parameter (e.g., /groups/999) to view or edit another group. Middleware verifies that authenticated user possesses explicit active Membership in target group for every request.
SQL Injection Unsanitized search fields altering backend SQL statements. Prisma ORM generates 100% parameterized SQL queries; manual raw query strings prohibited in production code.
Ledger Tampering by Rogue Treasurer Treasurer altering past contribution figures to conceal cash shortfalls. Database tables enforce append-only policies; financial mutations executed strictly via audited Adjustment records; zero in-place updates.

16.2 Cryptographic & Session Specifications

  • Password Storage: Salted and hashed using Argon2id with 64 MB memory cost, 3 iterations, and parallelism of 1 (fallback: bcrypt with 12 salt rounds).
  • Session Lifecycle: Session tokens are cryptographically random 256-bit entropy strings with a 7-day expiration sliding window. Inactive sessions terminate after 24 hours.
  • Audit Log Immutability: The AuditLog table has no application-level update or delete endpoints. A PostgreSQL trigger can optionally raise an exception if an UPDATE or DELETE SQL statement is attempted on this table.

17. Financial Transaction Integrity, Reconciliation, and Reporting

17.1 Mathematical Precision Standards

Financial applications must never suffer from IEEE-754 floating-point rounding errors (where 0.1 + 0.2 = 0.30000000000000004). EthioEqub mandates:

  • Storage: All currency amounts are stored as 64-bit signed integers (BIGINT in PostgreSQL) representing minor currency units (santim / cents).
  • Formulas: $$\text{Stored Santim} = \text{ETB Amount} \times 100$$ $$\text{Displayed ETB} = \frac{\text{Stored Santim}}{100.00}$$
  • Arithmetic: All additions, subtractions, and balance summations occur strictly on integer values. Division is restricted to final payout split distributions with explicit integer remainder allocation.

17.2 Transactional Atomicity via ACID Guarantees

Every financial event spanning multiple records (e.g., recording a contribution, updating the cycle running balance, and inserting an audit log entry) is wrapped in a PostgreSQL atomic transaction: 

// Atomic Financial Execution Pattern (Prisma Transaction)
await prisma.$transaction(async (tx) => {
  // 1. Verify group is active and not frozen
  const group = await tx.equbGroup.findUniqueOrThrow({ where: { id: groupId } });
  if (group.status !== 'ACTIVE') throw new DomainError('GROUP_FROZEN');

  // 2. Prevent duplicate reference within group
  if (referenceNumber) {
    const existing = await tx.contribution.findFirst({
      where: { groupId, paymentMethod, referenceNumber }
    });
    if (existing) throw new ConflictError('DUPLICATE_PAYMENT_REFERENCE');
  }

  // 3. Persist contribution record
  const contribution = await tx.contribution.create({
    data: { membershipId, cycleId, amountMinorUnits, paymentMethod, referenceNumber, recordedById }
  });

  // 4. Persist immutable audit log entry
  await tx.auditLog.create({
    data: {
      actorId: recordedById,
      entityType: 'Contribution',
      entityId: contribution.id,
      action: 'RECORD_CONTRIBUTION',
      afterState: contribution,
      ipAddress, userAgent
    }
  });

  return contribution;
});

17.3 Automated Pool Reconciliation Invariant

At the conclusion of every cycle, the system validates the fundamental Equb conservation law:

The Equb Invariant Equation
$$\sum_{i=1}^{M} \text{Contributions}_c(i) + \sum \text{Adjustments}_c = \text{Payout}_c + \text{Carried Reserve}_c$$ Where $M$ is total group membership and $c$ is the current cycle index. If the calculated delta does not equal zero, the system flags a reconciliation variance, preventing cycle closure until rectified by the Treasurer.

26. Glossary of Cultural and Technical Terms

Term Definition and Context
Equb (ዕቁብ) Traditional Ethiopian Rotating Savings and Credit Association (ROSCA) wherein members pool periodic fixed contributions and distribute the accumulated sum to one winner per cycle.
Sebsabi (ሰብሳቢ) The elected chairman, organizer, or administrative owner of an Equb group responsible for governance, rules, and dispute oversight.
Genzeb Yazhi (ገንዘብ ያዥ) The trusted treasurer of the Equb group responsible for collecting member payments, maintaining the ledger, and issuing receipts.
Wasa (ዋስ) A legal or community guarantor who pledges financial liability if a member defaults on their post-payout contribution obligations.
Cycle (ዙር) A single scheduled operational iteration (daily, weekly, or monthly) consisting of contribution collection and one payout disbursement.
Payout (ዕጣ) The aggregated sum of member contributions collected during a cycle and awarded to the designated beneficiary.
Minor Units The smallest non-divisible fractional denomination of a currency (e.g. santim for Ethiopian Birr; 1 ETB = 100 santim). Stored as integers to prevent floating-point calculation errors.
ROSCA Rotating Savings and Credit Association: an international peer-to-peer financial model recognized globally.
Dual Control A mandatory security governance policy requiring two independent authorized individuals to sign off before a sensitive financial action can execute.
ACID Atomicity, Consistency, Isolation, Durability: database guarantees ensuring that financial transactions are processed reliably without partial state corruption.
RBAC Role-Based Access Control: an authorization mechanism restricting system access based on assigned user roles (Admin, Treasurer, Manager, Member).
Argon2id The premier modern password hashing algorithm, resistant to GPU/ASIC cracking and side-channel timing attacks.
Idempotency An API property ensuring that submitting the same request multiple times produces the identical outcome without duplicate transactions.
Zod A TypeScript-first schema declaration and runtime data validation library.
Prisma A next-generation Object-Relational Mapper (ORM) for Node.js and TypeScript providing type-safe database queries and automated migrations.
